# Skill: Job Application Email Writer

## Purpose

Write concise, natural, human-sounding emails for job applications and recruiter/headhunter outreach.

The goal is NOT to rewrite the candidate's resume. The goal is to make the recruiter quickly understand:
1. Why the candidate is relevant to this specific role.
2. Which 2–4 parts of their experience match the position.
3. That the candidate is genuinely interested and worth contacting.

## Golden Rule: Analyze First, Write Second

Do not start writing immediately after receiving a job description.

First extract the 3–5 strongest signals from the vacancy and map them against the candidate's actual experience. Then write the email around the strongest overlap.

The email should feel like:
"Here is why my background is relevant to your specific problem."

It should NOT feel like:
"Here is my resume in paragraph form."

## Candidate Facts: Source and Selection

Treat the supplied `baseProfile` as the only source of candidate facts. Build a
short evidence map before writing: vacancy signal → matching profile excerpt.
Use only matches supported by that map, and omit facts that are merely plausible.

Important:
- Do not rely on examples, prior applications, vacancy wording, or this guide as candidate evidence.
- Do not turn a vacancy requirement into candidate experience.
- When a required technology is absent, describe a genuinely documented adjacent skill only when useful and state the gap honestly.
- Do not invent metrics, achievements, responsibilities, technologies, company names, language proficiency, or years of experience.

## Analyze the Job Before Writing

Before drafting the email, identify:

- Main technologies.
- Seniority.
- Main responsibilities.
- Most important requirements.
- Industry/domain if relevant.
- Whether the recipient is a recruiter, hiring manager, headhunter or generic hiring team.
- Any information that should be addressed directly.

Then select only the candidate's experience that is most relevant.

Do not mention every matching technology.

## Email Style

The email should sound like it was written by an experienced software engineer, not by an AI or a corporate copywriter.

Preferred characteristics:

- Natural.
- Direct.
- Professional.
- Conversational but not casual.
- Confident without exaggeration.
- Specific to the vacancy.
- Easy for a recruiter to scan.
- B1–B2/C1 English, depending on the context.
- Usually 150–250 words.
- Short paragraphs.
- No unnecessary buzzwords.

Avoid phrases such as:

- "I am thrilled to apply..."
- "I am extremely passionate about..."
- "I am excited about the opportunity..."
- "I believe I would be the perfect candidate..."
- "I am confident that my unique skill set..."
- "I bring a wealth of experience..."
- "I am eager to leverage my expertise..."
- "I look forward to contributing to your company's success..."
- "results-driven professional"
- "highly motivated"
- "dynamic environment"
- "proven track record"

Do not use exaggerated self-descriptions such as:

- "expert"
- "guru"
- "rockstar"
- "top-tier engineer"
- "world-class"

unless the recruiter/job description itself uses that terminology and there is a specific reason to reference it.

## Structure

Use this general structure:

1. Opening
   - Clearly state why you are contacting them.
   - Mention the exact position.
   - If a recruiter/headhunter posted the role, address them by name.

2. Relevant experience
   - Give a concise description of the candidate's background.
   - Prioritize the experience most relevant to this position.

3. Specific match
   - Mention 2–4 technologies, responsibilities or experiences that directly match the job.
   - Prefer concrete experience over generic claims.

4. Closing
   - Mention the CV/resume if attached.
   - Express interest in discussing the opportunity.
   - Keep the closing simple.

## Recruiter/Headhunter Emails

When contacting a recruiter or headhunter:

- Address the person by name when available.
- Do not write "Dear Hiring Team" if a specific recruiter is known.
- Make the first paragraph immediately explain why the candidate is relevant.
- Do not write a long biography.
- Do not repeat the entire resume.
- Make it easy for the recruiter to forward the email to the hiring manager.

## Subject Lines

Create a simple, professional subject.

Preferred formats:

"Application: [Exact Role Title] – [Candidate Name]"

"Application – [Exact Role Title] – [Candidate Name]"

"[Exact Role Title] – Application – [Candidate Name]"

Avoid:

"[Role] – [Unsupported promotional self-label]"

because it sounds promotional and self-labeled.

## Important Accuracy Rule

Never blindly copy requirements from the job description and present them as the candidate's experience.

If the vacancy requests a skill absent from the profile, do not copy it into the
candidate's experience. If the profile contains a relevant adjacent skill, name
the documented skill and distinguish it from the unmet requirement. Otherwise,
omit the claim and leave the gap explicit.

## Personalization

Every email should contain at least one detail that clearly connects the candidate to the specific vacancy.

For example:

If the job emphasizes production support:
→ emphasize production troubleshooting, monitoring and performance optimization.

If it emphasizes PostgreSQL:
→ emphasize PostgreSQL query optimization, indexing, migrations and database troubleshooting.

If it emphasizes legacy Rails:
→ emphasize Rails modernization, upgrades, dependency removal and maintaining existing systems.

If it emphasizes APIs:
→ emphasize REST APIs and integrations.

If it emphasizes AWS:
→ mention relevant AWS services actually used.

Do not add irrelevant technologies just to make the email look more impressive.

## Final Quality Check

Before returning the email, check:

- Does it sound human?
- Is it specific to this job?
- Is every technical claim accurate?
- Does it avoid exaggeration?
- Does it avoid generic AI/corporate language?
- Is it concise enough for a recruiter?
- Does it highlight the strongest relevant experience?
- Does it avoid repeating the resume?
- Does the subject line sound natural?
- If a recruiter is named, is the email addressed to them?
- Does the email make the candidate sound like a senior engineer rather than someone trying too hard to sell themselves?

Return:
1. The recommended subject.
2. The final email.
3. A brief note explaining the main personalization choices.
